Functional English
for Engineers

A six-week workplace communication curriculum for the Global Engineering Talent (GET) Program. The course outcomes and weekly materials connect English development with authentic professional tasks: networking, conflict resolution, WHS communication, analytical writing, influence and technical presentations.

Six-week sequenceOnline deliveryEngineering workplace contextSession plans · presentations · applied tasks

Design brief

The course outcomes define a broad performance target: help engineering learners communicate, collaborate and innovate in Australian workplace settings. The session-plan collection shows a practical online delivery model using trainer input, breakout-room collaboration, role-play, discussion, written work and feedback.

Audience & contextEngineering professionals developing workplace English for Australian and multinational contexts.
Performance focusCommunicate technical concerns and proposals clearly to colleagues, managers and stakeholders.
Delivery evidenceWeek-by-week session plans, lesson presentations, learner handouts and realistic workplace scenarios.

Learning progression

The sequence combines language input with rehearsal and increasingly authentic performance. Learners are asked to explain, justify, respond and improve—not only recall terminology.

01 · Build repertoire

Explore communication strategies, genre conventions and language for workplace interaction.

02 · Analyse situations

Interpret audience needs, conflict, risk, evidence and organisational context.

03 · Rehearse performance

Collaborate in discussions, practise difficult conversations and prepare written or spoken messages.

04 · Demonstrate & refine

Deliver a presentation or report, receive feedback and identify concrete next steps.

Design decisions visible in the materials

Use job-relevant scenarios

Communication practice is situated in engineering workplaces, including WHS reporting and discussion with superiors, so learners rehearse consequential language in context.

Vary the performance mode

Written communication, discussion, role-play, presentation and reflection let learners practise the same professional capabilities across different formats.

Make feedback part of the sequence

Peer critique, debriefing and presentation review create opportunities to interpret feedback and revise performance, not just complete a one-off task.

Attend to audience and purpose

Week 5 materials explicitly connect communication choices to stakeholder needs, audience knowledge, organisational position and confidentiality.
